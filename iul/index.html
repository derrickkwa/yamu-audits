01 — Business Snapshot

$22K/month in Meta spend, 685 customers over nine months, running two static images with no conversion infrastructure.

The market research points to a structural trust problem in the IUL lead category: the word "exclusive" is near-universal in vendor claims, but agent community forums (r/InsuranceAgent, insurance Facebook groups) document repeated instances of agents calling "exclusive" leads and reaching prospects who had already heard from multiple other agents. IUL.org's price point ($0.50/lead versus a category range of $37–$200+) and pre-education mechanism (co-reg leads email-educated on IUL basics before agent contact) are positioned differently from this norm. The platform data — 685 customers acquired over nine months at $22K/month in April 2026 — indicates the offer is finding buyers. Whether the pre-education mechanism drives materially better agent close rates compared to standard co-reg leads has not been formally measured.

The offer's proof points — and their fragility

The pre-educated leads angle is IUL.org's actual differentiator — leads who have received email education on IUL basics before being called. This is the mechanism that sets the product apart from commodity co-reg. But it relies on a claim that agents cannot verify independently, in a category where unverifiable claims are the norm.

The current conversion evidence (Stripe Verified Purchaser badges with real agent names) is the strongest signal in the market — but it's not independently audited. Two risks:

1. Community exposure: If any agent shares a substantiated negative experience in r/InsuranceAgent, the trust architecture has no buffer. A single credible negative post in a high-trust community can outweigh 800 positive testimonials in a category already primed for distrust.

2. TrustedForm gap: The TrustedForm certification is claimed throughout the landing page but the TrustedForm script is not installed. This is both a legal and credibility exposure. The FTC's $145M joint action against MediaAlpha and Assurance IQ (Aug 2025) put the entire lead generation industry on notice — the core charge was that consumers didn't understand how their data would be used. TrustedForm exists specifically to document consent at form-fill. Claiming it's in place when it isn't removes that proof layer at the exact moment it matters most: if scrutinized by a compliance-minded agency buyer, an FTC inquiry, or a competitor running opposition research.

The offer's strength is real. The fragility is that it rests on a single trust mechanic (Stripe badges) with no redundant proof layer. Agent testimonial video — even one strong case study — creates a second trust signal that can't be fabricated.

02 — Revenue You're Not Capturing

32.7% of payment attempts fail. $31.8K of that is recoverable this week.

Across 9 months of Stripe history (Aug 2025 – May 2026), IUL.org booked $161K gross / $137K net across 685 unique customers. The most consequential finding is operational: 32.7% of all card attempts fail, and an estimated $31.8K of that sits in soft-decline categories (insufficient funds, do-not-honor, try-again-later) — meaning the card could have processed with a retry. There is currently no retry infrastructure, no card-update prompt, and no SMS on decline. This is revenue sitting in Stripe with no system to catch it.

The dispute cliff — 10 basis points from Stripe review

24 disputes on record. Dispute rate: 0.78% of paid revenue. Stripe initiates account review at 0.9% — a threshold that's 0.12 percentage points away at current rates.

Of the 24 disputes, 12 cite "subscription_canceled" as the reason code — consistent with customers who did not locate a self-serve cancellation path and disputed the charge through their bank. One elevated renewal month could push the rate above 0.9%.

The recurring layer — what 71% participation actually means

71% of front-end customers trigger at least one auto-recharge of their GHL wallet. That sounds like strong recurring behavior. But the median recharger does exactly one recharge, with a median lifetime of 0 days — they top up once and disappear.

Auto-recharges contribute 8.4% of total revenue, not the 30%+ the high participation rate implies. The wallet credits are too small ($10 median) and customer engagement decays too fast to compound.

The recurring layer exists but is not currently a meaningful revenue driver. The unlock is a post-purchase email sequence (7/14/30 days) that re-engages customers when their leads have been consumed — timed to the natural refill cycle rather than arbitrary cadence.

The recurring revenue ceiling is not a product problem — leads get consumed and agents need more. It's a timing problem. The sequence needs to catch customers at the moment of depletion, not at an arbitrary 30-day mark.

Stripe's transaction data indicates three addressable issues: soft-decline revenue without retry infrastructure ($31.8K estimated recoverable), a dispute rate 12 basis points from platform review threshold, and a right-skewed LTV distribution where the median customer ($111) sits well below the mean ($194.83). Each has a corresponding platform-level fix — Stripe Smart Retries, pre-renewal notifications, and dispute response — that doesn't depend on changes to creative or acquisition strategy.

03 — Attribution Analysis

Meta self-reported 114 April purchases. Stripe recorded 273 new customers in the same period.

Comparing Meta Ads Manager's reported purchase count (114, April 2026) against Stripe's new customer count (273, April 2026) produces a 139% gap. The discrepancy points to a misconfigured or missing server-side conversion signal: CAPI (Conversions API) is not installed, the Purchase pixel event fires on /order page load rather than on confirmed transaction, and all five Meta attribution windows (1-day click, 7-day click, 28-day click, 1-day view, 7-day view) return identical purchase counts — a pattern consistent with a collapsed or single-fire event that doesn't distinguish attribution window timing. Advanced Matching status is unconfirmed. Domain verification in Business Manager is unconfirmed. The result is that Meta's optimization algorithm has been receiving a partial signal; the campaign bidding, creative performance rankings, and CPA figures in Ads Manager reflect approximately 42% of actual transactions.

Why all five attribution windows return the same number

In a properly configured Meta account, the 1-day-click window shows fewer conversions than 7-day-click, which shows fewer than 28-day-click. Different windows capture different attribution signals — and the counts should differ accordingly.

The fact that all five windows return identical purchase counts indicates the Purchase event is being attributed to a single window regardless of actual click/view timing. This is consistent with a missing or misconfigured CAPI setup where the pixel fires once on page load rather than being triggered by the actual purchase event.

Until CAPI is configured with deduplication on event_id, every optimization decision is based on a collapsed signal. The algorithm cannot distinguish between a click that converted in 1 day and one that converted after 28 days — both look identical in the current setup.

This is also why the Meta-reported 626 April purchases differs from the 114 unique customer count — the pixel is likely counting duplicate or session-level fires rather than unique conversion events. CAPI with deduplication resolves both the gap and the window-collapse problem simultaneously.

No behavioral data (session recordings, heatmaps) is currently collected — Clarity and GA4 are not installed — so diagnosing the specific drop-off point within the landing page requires instrumentation before any CRO changes can be evaluated against baseline.
What the data suggests

The attribution gap (Meta 114 vs. Stripe 273) indicates the optimization algorithm is working from incomplete data. Closing it via CAPI with server-side event deduplication is a prerequisite to trusting any subsequent creative performance reads or CPA benchmarks. Until that signal is clean and stable for a sufficient window, comparative performance metrics across creatives or campaigns should be interpreted with caution.

The deleted campaign that worked — and why it matters

"IUL.org/Promo - Calendar Acuity" delivered a $5.30 cost per purchase on $196 spend before being deleted. That's the lowest CPA in the entire account history — lower than any current active campaign.

The creative was different: it linked to a Calendly/Acuity booking flow rather than the direct purchase page. This was killed without being properly scaled or investigated.

If the booking-flow approach converts at $5.30 CPA, it represents a fundamentally different funnel architecture worth testing at real budget. A booked consultation model also changes the sales dynamic — it creates a commitment event before payment, which could reduce disputes and improve LTV by ensuring only qualified agents reach the checkout.

The campaign was deleted before sufficient data accumulated to assess statistical significance or structural repeatability. The $5.30 CPA figure warrants investigation — understanding why it was stopped and whether the booking-flow mechanism was the driver would inform whether the approach is worth replicating.

Category trust dynamics — the "exclusive" claim problem

Agent community research (r/InsuranceAgent, insurance Facebook groups, May 2026) documents a recurring pattern: agents purchasing "exclusive" leads from multiple vendors and reaching prospects who have already been called by other agents. The market research describes this as a structural credibility problem for the category term "exclusive" — the claim appears across nearly all vendor marketing but is frequently contradicted by buyer experience.

IUL.org uses Stripe Verified Purchaser badges on testimonials — meaning the purchase record, not a self-reported claim, is the source of verification. No other active competitor in the share-of-search dataset appears to use transaction-level verification on buyer testimonials. Whether agents distinguish this from standard testimonials in practice has not been tested.

The market research also notes that vendor recommendations circulate in agent communities primarily through peer-to-peer channels — agents recommending to other agents — rather than through vendor-owned content. Caboom's branded search growth correlates with increased participation in those channels.

The share-of-search data suggests that community trust (agent-to-agent recommendation) is the mechanism Caboom used to grow branded search 14× over three years. IUL.org's Stripe Verified Purchaser proof infrastructure is a differentiated asset — whether it translates to the same mechanism depends on whether it reaches agent communities, not just paid media audiences.

06 — Recommendations

Q2 — attribution, tracking, and creative test. Q3 — channel expansion, creative system, and LTV stack.

The findings across the four preceding sections point to two sequencing priorities. The first is measurement: the attribution gap (Section 03), the absence of behavioral analytics, and the uncollected soft-decline revenue (Section 02) are foundational issues whose resolution makes subsequent decisions more reliable. The second is creative: the current two-image portfolio shows frequency and cost-per-purchase trends consistent with saturation (Section 04), and without new creative inventory, spend efficiency will continue to deteriorate regardless of tracking improvements. The Q2 recommendations address both in parallel. Q3 recommendations are contingent on Q2 outcomes — specifically, clean attribution data for 14+ days and at least one creative angle with measurable CPA data.

Q2 creative recommendation — 4-angle demand point test

The existing data points to three angles with positive ROAS signals (unit economics, pre-educated mechanism, and income/proof framing) and one with a strongly negative signal (discount framing). A structured 4-angle test run simultaneously at equal budget ($50/day each) would produce comparable creative performance data for the first time, using the existing workhorse statics as a control baseline. The unit economics angle (B) should remain live as the highest observed ROAS until an angle definitively outperforms it in a controlled comparison.
