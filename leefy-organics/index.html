01 — Attribution

The 1.30x reported ROAS breaks down to 0.78x on a true incremental basis. Every isolated attribution window is below breakeven.

GA4 first-click attribution (property 396523834) shows campaign-level ROAS of 0.25x–0.43x across the largest campaigns.

ROAS trend — 43% decline from early 2025 to early 2026
Meta ROAS by Month (May 2025 – May 2026)

Meta monthly-trends data shows ROAS averaged 1.64x in May–Sep 2025, peaked at 3.10x in October 2025 (Black Friday prep, lowest spend month at $5,688), then declined to 0.84–0.88x in Mar–Apr 2026. The peak coincided with the lowest spend and highest creative efficiency — the MJ batch running at $16.30 CPM vs the current $32.71 average. The declining trend reflects increasing spend into a stale creative pool, not a loss of category demand.

MMM channel attribution — model confidence LOW due to channel co-launch

The Media Mix Model (PyMC + Robyn, 124 weeks of Shopify revenue data) was run to assess true channel contribution. Both models converged technically (PyMC max R-hat 1.002, Robyn RSSD 0.0021) but diverge substantially on channel splits — Meta: 45% (PyMC) vs 76% (Robyn); Google: 55% (PyMC) vs 24% (Robyn). This divergence reflects a fundamental data limitation: both channels launched simultaneously in late 2024 and scaled together (r = 0.53 collinearity), making clean attribution separation unreliable.

What the data suggests

The 1.30x Ads Manager figure is a combination of real-click attribution and view-through attribution counted simultaneously. With 42.9% of default-window revenue attributed to view-through, and every isolated click window below breakeven, the account's true economic performance is materially below what the dashboard headline shows. The incrementality model (0.78x) and GA4 first-click (0.25–0.43x) represent the floor and directional ceiling of true incremental returns. The gap between 7-day click (0.82x) and incrementality (0.78x) is narrow — Meta's own causal estimate closely tracks the click-only window, suggesting the 7-day click ROAS is the most reliable operational metric for this account.

03 — Creative Performance

Sensory and convenience-framed ads deliver 2.47× the ROAS of clinical framing, yet clinical angles receive 19% of budget and sensory angles receive 7%.

Meta creative-feature analysis and ad-breakdown data (May 2025–May 2026) segments ad performance by hook type and visual angle. The "sublingual absorption" and clinical bioavailability framing, which leads with the mechanism of action in scientific terms, averages 0.70x ROAS across ads on that angle. VoC signal — 22 unanswered "turmeric didn't work" objections in ad comments

VoC analysis of ad comments (Meta VoC agent, May 2026) surfaced 22 instances of the objection "I've tried turmeric before and it didn't work" or close variants across comment threads. The brand reply rate to this specific objection is 0%. This objection represents the primary conversion barrier for a product that works via a differentiated delivery mechanism — the turmeric capsule failure is the exact reason liquid tincture absorption delivers different results. Other users reading the same threads observe the unanswered objection.

Additional VoC signals: multiple self-identified pregnant and postpartum women engaging with inflammation and brain-fog content, with zero dedicated creative currently running to this segment. VoC also shows a self-selected verification-first buyer profile requesting COA documentation and third-party testing transparency — a demand point that zero competitors are addressing in paid ads.

UGC performance context — high spend, conversion-objective framing

The account carries significant UGC spend across the "05 | Prana Product" campaign (98 active ads, $29,334 spend, 1.12x ROAS). UGC creative at 1.12x underperforms the account's static-format winners (3.88x, 3.62x). The performance gap is not purely a creative quality issue — the adstock analysis (0.8–0.9 week half-life on Meta) indicates that regardless of format, the current campaign objective configuration (conversion-optimized, ROAS bidding) directs spend toward audiences most likely to convert immediately. This means UGC is performing the same function as catalog ads: capturing in-market demand, not building memory or reach for problem-aware buyers. Format changes alone will not change this — campaign objective and bidding strategy determine who sees the content.

What the data suggests

The creative angle producing 2.47x the ROAS of the account's dominant angle is running at approximately 7% of budget. The angle at 0.70x ROAS — clinical sublingual framing — is running at 19%. The static-format winners (3.88x, 3.62x, 3.10x) share three characteristics: sensory or convenience lead, informational format, and structured ABO test methodology. The VoC objection data suggests there is a high-intent warm audience in comments actively signaling purchase consideration that is currently receiving no brand response.

04 — Google Ads

Google delivers 1.74x ROAS on $11,594 spend, but Smart Goal is co-primary alongside purchase events — diluting Smart Bidding's optimization signal on every active campaign.

Google Ads data covers the 90-day period to May 2026. Account ROAS of 1.74x ($11,594 spend → $20,186 revenue, 320 conversions) is above the account average but the conversion tracking audit reveals two CRITICAL flags: Smart Goal (HIDDEN, ENGAGEMENT category, Included=True, Primary=True) is included as a primary conversion action alongside Google Shopping App Purchase (PURCHASE, Included=True, Primary=True). Smart Bidding uses all primary conversion signals simultaneously — a co-primary engagement proxy trains the algorithm away from purchase maximization.

What the data suggests

The branded search data tells a specific story: paid media is currently generating impressions without building sustained brand awareness. The adstock carryover of <1 week means spend creates near-zero residual memory week-over-week. Combined with the market-state finding — the buyer population most likely to search "leefy organics" (solution-aware, category-specific) is in structural decline, while the buyer population searching "antioxidant supplement" or "gut health supplement" is growing fast — the brand's current media architecture is optimised for a shrinking pool of buyers. The Market Sophistication Index of 0.014 means 71 problem-aware searches occur for every 1 solution-seeking search: the market has not consolidated around greens powder as the answer to these health concerns. This is both the challenge (harder to convert) and the opportunity (unclaimed territory, no dominant solution-layer brand for problem-aware buyers).
