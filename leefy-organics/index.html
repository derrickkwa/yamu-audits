01 — The Category Has Shifted

The pool of buyers ready to purchase a greens powder today is contracting −23.7% YoY. The pool of buyers with a health problem and no settled solution is up +390%.

Market-state analysis covers 55 keyword groups tracked monthly via DataForSEO (May 2026). The data reveals a structural divergence in the supplement market: searches for specific product types ("greens powder", "superfood supplement") are declining as the AG1-era hype peak deflates, while problem-layer searches — antioxidants, gut health, immune support — are surging. These two pools require fundamentally different marketing. The current Leefy media architecture is built exclusively for the first, shrinking pool.

03 — The Brand Is Not Building Memory

Paid media creates impressions with a less than 1-week half-life. People see Leefy ads, don't convert immediately, and forget about the brand within days — because there's nothing to bring them back.

Media Mix Modelling (PyMC + Robyn, 124 weeks of Shopify revenue data) measured adstock carryover — the degree to which this week's ad exposure creates awareness that persists into future weeks. Both models show a 0.8–0.9 week half-life on Meta and Google. Attribution window data from Meta confirms this from a different angle: the 28-day click window adds only 5% more purchases over the 7-day click window, meaning people either convert within a week of seeing an ad or don't return at all.

The dominant opening hook across the current creative library is "claim" — a benefit assertion ("This supplement supports joint health", "Clinically formulated for absorption") — accounting for 73.9% of spend. Claim-first formats are built for buyers already in an evaluation mindset: they present a reason to buy and wait for a yes or no decision. They produce no residual impression in a buyer who isn't ready to decide this week.

The formats that build memory work differently. Sensory and emotive creative — the dropper into water, the taste moment, the daily ritual — attach the product to an experience or feeling the viewer can recall later, without requiring an immediate decision. The performance data reflects this: the three highest-performing individual ads in the account (3.88x, 3.62x, 3.10x ROAS) are all sensory or emotive formats. The clinical claim-first angle averages 0.70x. The adstock finding — less than 1-week half-life on both channels — is consistent with a library built almost entirely on claim-first hooks that leave nothing behind when the ad stops running.

MMM saturation — both channels have significant headroom

The MMM grid search found a saturation parameter of k = 0.01 for both Meta and Google — the lowest value tested, indicating the saturation curve is essentially flat at current spend levels. Neither channel is anywhere near its capacity ceiling at $2,147/week combined. The MMM estimates organic and baseline channels (email, direct, brand, Amazon-to-DTC) account for ~78% of Shopify web revenue — a resilient foundation built on genuine product-market fit. Paid media accounts for ~22% of web revenue on a modest $9,300/month budget, suggesting meaningful room to scale on the paid side before diminishing returns.

The adstock finding and the brand search decline confirm the same thing from different angles: impressions either convert in the week they run or leave nothing behind. 04 — The True Economics

The 1.30x Ads Manager ROAS breaks down to 0.78x on a true incremental basis. Every isolated attribution window is below breakeven.

Meta Ads Manager reports a 12-month blended ROAS of 1.30x ($164,053 spend → $212,926 revenue, 3,546 purchases). The Attribution tab shows this figure combines multiple windows simultaneously. When each window is isolated, no single one replicates the 1.30x headline — and every isolated window, including Meta's own incrementality model, is below 1.0x. GA4 first-click attribution (property 396523834) shows 0.25x–0.43x ROAS across the largest campaigns.

The default window (7D click + 1D view) is 3,546 purchases vs 7-day click at 2,478 — a gap of 1,068 purchases and $78,289 in revenue, which is exactly the 1-day view increment. That 36.8% gap is what separates the reported 1.30x from the click-only 0.82x. Meta's incrementality model returns 0.78x — nearly identical to the 7-day click figure, not close to the default. This suggests the view-through purchases are largely not causal: holdout testing doesn't find them as incremental, meaning they likely would have happened without the ad impression. Meta is optimizing toward the combined 1.30x signal, which means Smart Bidding is partly learning to target people who show the view-through pattern — users already deep in the purchase funnel from another channel who get served an impression just before checkout. The 28-day click window adds only 125 purchases over the 7-day window (5% lift), consistent with what the adstock data shows: people buy within the week of clicking or don't return.

ROAS trend — 43% decline from early 2025 to early 2026
Meta in-platform ROAS by month (May 2025 – Apr 2026)

ROAS averaged 1.64x in May–Sep 2025, peaked at 3.10x in October 2025 (lowest spend month at $5,688, highest creative discipline — the MJ ABO batch running at $16.30 CPM), then declined to 0.84–0.88x in Mar–Apr 2026 as spend increased into a stale creative pool. The October peak shows the account is capable of strong returns with the right creative-audience match. The declining trajectory reflects budget scaling without creative evolution, not loss of market demand.

Why platform ROAS is not the right optimization target

Platform ROAS and MER ratios are diagnostics, not optimization targets. A 3.0x MER on $200k spend can generate more absolute profit than a 5.0x MER on $50k spend — if the marginal return on incremental spend remains above the cost of capital. The MMM saturation analysis shows k = 0.01 on both channels: neither is near its efficiency ceiling at current spend levels. The relevant question is whether the next dollar of spend returns more than it costs in absolute contribution margin — not whether a ratio improves or degrades. Both channels are directionally under-saturated, suggesting the brand may be underspending relative to the profit-maximizing point on current infrastructure, independent of the attribution questions.

What the data suggests

The gap between the default window (1.30x) and the 7-day click window (0.82x) is 36.8% — and that gap is the 1-day view component. Meta's incrementality model returns 0.78x, nearly identical to the click-only figure. This suggests most of the view-through volume is not causal: people who were served an impression shortly before purchasing through another path — organic, email, direct — with the ad having little effect on the decision. Because Meta optimizes toward the default 1.30x signal, Smart Bidding may be partly learning to find people already in the purchase funnel rather than incremental new buyers. The 7-day click ROAS (0.82x) is the more reliable signal, and it is below breakeven.
