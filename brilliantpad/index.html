Revenue has held flat at ~$73–78K/month only because the January 2024 cohort (677 customers at 71.4% repeat rate) is still cycling through roll purchases. When those customers' subscriptions complete, there is no replacement volume behind them.

This is not a slow decline — it's a math problem with a deadline. At the current trajectory, BrilliantPad will have fewer than 400 active customers by end of 2026. The monthly revenue from 400 customers at 71.4% retention cannot sustain $75K/month. The acquisition restart is not a growth initiative. It's a survival prerequisite.

What we'd do

Restart acquisition in two phases: immediate — Google brand + rolls retargeting (both proven, both measurable without attribution infrastructure) + breeder/PuppySpot expansion; month 2–3 — Meta relaunch once attribution is clean. The priority is not scale. It's restarting the pipeline before the 2024 cohort's roll cycles end.

The "unknown" bucket almost certainly contains organic direct traffic, subscription repeat orders, and Amazon-to-D2C conversion — sources where UTM parameters are missing or lost. This isn't just an analytics problem. Any paid media investment at meaningful scale right now will produce ROAS numbers that claim credit for organic conversions, creating a false signal that causes either premature scale or premature kill decisions. Both outcomes have played out in BrilliantPad's Google history.

The attribution gap is the prerequisite, not the afterthought. Every previous agency likely encountered this and tried to optimize on dirty data. Fixing this before scaling paid media is what separates a restartable growth model from another failed scale attempt.
What we'd do

Four-week infrastructure sprint: confirm GA4 purchase events match Shopify order count (currently divergent by estimated 20%+); deploy UTM tagging on all paid sources including email, breeder links, and affiliate; reconcile the "unknown" bucket — identify what share is subscription repeat vs. organic vs. untagged. Target: <40% unknown before Meta launches. Paid media starts the moment that bar is cleared, not before.

The two ICPs that convert and retain

Every prior campaign targeted all dog owners. The data points to two segments that work — and nothing else.

ICP 1 — Existing pad users (pad upgraders)

Already spending money on flat pads. Already managing the problem. BrilliantPad is an upgrade, not a new category to evaluate. 83% consumable attach in targeted cohorts. The pad-upgrader creative hook ("You already use pads — here's one you never touch again") is completely unclaimed by every competitor.

Addressable audience: 25K–80K/month searching flat-pad terms. The acquisition channel already exists — it's just never been messaged to with ICP precision.
ICP 2 — New small-breed puppy owners

During the vet-mandated vaccination window, these owners have a forced indoor potty need with zero outdoor option. The timing creates near-perfect purchase intent. PuppySpot is already delivering this exact audience at zero CAC with 93% new customer rate. Alan's list of 7–8 target breeds narrows this further.

Addressable channel: Breeder partnerships deliver this ICP at acquisition for zero marginal CAC. Currently underscaled at 1 partnership.
What we'd do

Make the bundle the explicit acquisition offer: hardware + first roll shipment at subscription-committed pricing. 30.4% of customers already do this naturally — formalizing it as the primary offer starts the subscription relationship at acquisition, rather than hoping post-purchase flows convert hardware buyers later. Test bundle vs. hardware-only as a head-to-head — measure 90-day repeat rate, not first-purchase ROAS.

The post-purchase machine flow has 67 email opens — meaning the emails fire and get read. The $0 revenue means the CTA is broken (wrong URL, broken link, or dead offer). This is an execution bug, not an audience problem. It is the highest-ROI fix in the entire Klaviyo account.
Why the post-purchase machine flow matters so much

Hardware buyers have a 41% repeat rate. Auto-delivery roll subscribers have an 89% repeat rate. That 48-point gap represents $261 in LTV per hardware buyer who never subscribes to rolls. The post-purchase machine flow — a 3-email sequence that introduces the roll subscription, explains the cost-per-use economics, and presents auto-delivery as the obvious next step — is the mechanism that closes this gap. It's already built. It's already live. The only thing broken is the CTA link. Fixing it is a 30-minute task with potentially hundreds of dollars per customer in downstream LTV impact.

The replenishment flow: $544 on $224K in roll orders

The roll replenishment flow — designed to remind non-subscriber hardware buyers to reorder — is enrolling only 122 people per 90-day period while $224K in roll orders flows through the account. This means the flow's trigger audience is too narrow or misconfigured. Every hardware buyer without an active auto-delivery subscription should be in this flow within 30 days of purchase. At $224K in annual roll revenue, even a 5% capture improvement is worth ~$11K/year in directly attributable email revenue.

9,317 dormant subscribers — how to reactivate without burning the list

In February 2026, a broadcast campaign to a broad segment produced a 2.7% unsubscribe rate — 9× above threshold. The list has been dark since. The right reactivation path: resume to 90-day engaged openers only (those who opened an email in the last 90 days — a fraction of the 9,317). Build a sunset suppression flow to filter long-inactive subscribers before they can damage deliverability. Monthly cadence: roll subscription pitch, Mini hardware announcement, seasonal triggers (spring puppy peak, holiday gifting). Never broad-blast the full list.

What we'd do

Week 1: Fix the post-purchase machine flow CTA — verify the link, test the offer, confirm revenue attribution. Week 2: Expand the replenishment flow trigger to catch all hardware buyers without active auto-delivery at 30 days. Week 5: Resume broadcast to 90-day engaged segment only. Governance audit of 95 draft flows before any new flow work — 95 drafts with no authoritative version is an execution liability.
